# Blueprint Generation

A Factorio 2.1 mod that builds blueprints for you. Pick an item and how many you want per minute, and the mod puts a ready-to-place blueprint on your cursor. The blueprint carries raw materials from a belt into machines, through every intermediate product on the way, and the finished product onto an output belt: ores in, final product out.

Status: early development.

## Using it

1. Click the "Generate blueprint" button in the shortcut bar.
2. Choose the recipe to produce, in the game's own picker of recipes, which shows the icons of your crafting menu (the recipe of iron plate is already selected, if you can build it), and enter a rate of what the recipe makes: in items per minute, or in units of the fluid a minute (see "A recipe that makes a fluid: basic oil processing"). The picker offers the recipes you have unlocked that the mod builds. The game cannot filter its picker by the name of a recipe, only by what a recipe makes, so a recipe that makes the same thing in another way (advanced oil processing makes petroleum gas too) shows in it as well, and is answered with a message under the rate field, as is a recipe that cannot be made for another reason. Nothing else about the recipe is looked at: the game never unlocks a recipe before those of its ingredients or before the crafting machine it needs. The buildings of the blueprint are checked where a better one is used once you have it (the fast belt, the medium electric pole, the fast inserter, the steel furnace, the assembling machine 2, the lamp and the display panel: see the sections below); the long-handed inserter, the transport belt, the inserter and the small electric pole are assumed, and a later feature will check them. Research something new and the message under the rate field becomes the maximum rate the next time you pick it.
3. Click Generate. The blueprint appears on your cursor. It is temporary, like a blueprint taken from the library: it takes no inventory space, and it disappears if you put it away without placing it (generate it again if that happens). Place it, put the raw items on the input belts and connect the poles to your power network. The message says how much the blueprint makes a minute and how much of each raw item (and of the fuel) it uses a minute, for example `Ready: 75 Iron plate/min`, then `Raw/min: 75 Iron ore, 5.4 Coal`. If you have researched the splitter and the underground belt, it also names the belts to bring in, from the top of the blueprint down, for example `Belts in, top to bottom: Iron ore, Coal` (see "One belt of each material").

The assembling machines come with their recipe already set; a furnace needs none. A blueprint is a row of columns of machines, one column per step from the raw items to the product, from left to right. A column is an input belt, inserters, the machines, output inserters and an output belt, and the output belt of one column is the input belt of the next. The belt between two columns runs toward the machines that still need its items, so none of the plates pass the second column by: up the page when the first column is the taller one, down when the second is. All the columns start on the same row. Iron plate, copper plate and stone brick take one column (furnaces fed with ore and coal). Iron gear wheel takes two: furnaces on the left make plates from iron ore, and assembling machines on the right make gear wheels from those plates. Small electric poles are placed and wired for you, and one wire joins each column to the next, so the whole blueprint needs one connection to your power network. The belts, inserters and poles are the first-tier ones (transport belt, inserter, small electric pole) unless your force has researched better ones: see "Fast belts", "Fast inserters" and "Medium electric poles".

### A step with one ingredient on top of a step with several inputs

Hazard concrete is made from concrete alone, and the concrete comes from several inputs (bricks, iron ore, water). The column of the hazard concrete machines stands right beside the column of the concrete machines, as the column after another in a chain of steps does: the belt between them is the concrete's output belt and the input belt of the hazard concrete, so the inserters of the first column drop the concrete onto it and those of the second take it. A step with one ingredient on top of one of several inputs can go on in a chain (a second step on top of that one). A furnace that burns fuel cannot take such a place (its fuel would have no belt of its own to ride), and neither can a step with a raw ingredient too, which is refused.

### Recipes with two made ingredients

An electronic circuit is an iron plate and three copper cables, and the cables are made from copper plates. The mod builds a chain of columns for each ingredient (the row of columns above, with its own belt of raw items) and puts the machines that eat them in a column of their own. So every step of the recipe is a column, and the columns stand side by side from left to right: the ones that make the ingredients first, then the one that eats them. A step can have steps of its own, as deep as the recipe goes (a transport belt is an iron plate and a gear, and a gear is made from iron plates).

**The recipe fixes the width of the blueprint, and the rate only makes it taller.** More items a minute means more machines in a column, never another column. The circuit is 26 tiles wide, the inserter 52, the fast inserter 91 and the fast splitter 110, whatever the rate; a low rate gives a flat strip, and you can turn a blueprint when you place it.

The belts of raw items come in from the west edge at the top, one row each, and the belt of the chain furthest east has the top row, so no belt crosses another. Each chain starts right under its own belt, so the columns do not start on one row. The product of a column leaves at its bottom, runs east along a row of its own and goes up into the input belt of the column that eats it, which runs north. An inserter drops on the far lane and a belt that feeds the side of another puts everything on the near lane, so the two ingredients ride different lanes, and the inserters of the new column take both: the second product joins the input belt from the west (the west lane), and the first goes through a small lane gadget, a belt that comes up into the side of its own row from the south (the south lane, which is the east lane of the input belt after the turn). The lane gadget is checked by the specs but has not been seen running in game yet. A column that eats what others make starts one row lower than the belts of raw items that pass over it, so its input belt does not end pointing into one of them. The poles of each column are wired to the nearest column, so the whole blueprint is one network.

### A raw item next to a made one

A small electric pole is a wood and two copper cables. The cables get a chain, as above. The wood has no recipe, so it needs no machines: its column is only a belt, with a spare column beside it for the lane gadget of the product before it. The belt comes in from the west edge on a row of its own like the others, runs down its column and along the bottom to the input belt, where the wood and the cables share the two lanes. The pole machines take both.

### Recipes with more than two ingredients

A belt has only two lanes, so a machine that eats more than two items takes them from more than one belt. A blueprint can do it in two ways, and takes the better one that your force has the buildings for.

#### Two belts and a long-handed inserter, from the start

A machine that eats three or four items takes them from two belts side by side: belt a, next to its inserters, and belt b, one column further west. A plain inserter takes from belt a, and a long-handed inserter, standing in the same column of inserters, reaches over belt a to take from belt b. Each machine gets one plain and one long-handed inserter (a machine has room for two inserters on its input side). Which ingredients go on which belt is decided for speed: a long-handed inserter moves 72 items a minute against 50.4 for a plain one, so the mod tries every split (at most two ingredients on a belt, a raw item only on belt a) and takes the one that lets a machine run fastest. An inserter, for example, wants 60 circuits, 60 gears and 60 plates a minute at full speed: with the circuits alone on belt a and the other two on belt b, an assembler makes 36 a minute; the other splits make 25.

The columns that make the ingredients stand from left to right: first those of belt a, then those of belt b, each in the recipe's order, then the final column, with a column of its own for belt b just west of it. Both belts run north and the products reach them from below, the first product of a belt through the lane gadget when a second one shares the belt. Belt b starts at the first long-handed inserter, so every inserter sees everything that comes up the belt. An arc of a column further west runs deeper than the arcs of the columns east of it, so belts never cross.

The long-handed inserter is unlocked by the same research as the assembling machine. The mod does not check that you have it. It uses the speed of the game's long-handed inserter (the plain inserter's if the game has none). Two belts are all that a long-handed inserter reaches, so an item with five or six ingredients is not built this way (see below).

#### Woven belts, once the splitter and the underground belt are researched

A machine that eats more than two items takes them from two or three belts, up to six ingredients. The belts are woven so that plain inserters (fast ones, once you have researched them) reach all of them, and no long-handed inserter is needed. The input side of a 3 x 3 machine takes up to three inserters, one in each row, in the column beside the machine (the inner column), and that column shows a different belt in each row:

- **Belt a** is an underground belt that goes under the rows of the others and comes up where its own inserters are.
- **Belt b** runs down a column of its own two columns west of the inner column, and a splitter peels a share off at the head of each run of rows of b: a tile that points east into the inner column, where the inserter takes from it. Both lanes stay as they are, since the splitter keeps them.
- **Belt c**, which five or six ingredients need (the planner uses it for fewer only when that makes the machines faster), runs down two columns further west, and its tap goes east under belt b to come up in the column between belt b and the inner column.

Which belt each row shows alternates machine by machine, so that two machines side by side show the same belt where they meet: `a b c | c b a` with one inserter on each of three belts, `a b a | a b a` with two inserters on belt a and one on b, `b b a | a b b` with two on belt b. The column between belt b and the inner column has the poles, in the tiles the pattern leaves free, so that the machines stand packed with no row of poles between them. The poles reach the inserters from there; three belts leave so few free tiles that only a pole that reaches three rows serves them (the medium pole), two belts also work with the small pole.

Which ingredients go on which belt is decided for speed: a machine has three input slots for its woven belts, shared between the belts, at least one each, and the mod tries every split (at most two ingredients on a belt, a raw item only on belt a) and every share of the slots, and takes the one that lets a machine run fastest, on as few belts as it takes. An inserter, for example, wants 60 circuits, 60 gears and 60 plates a minute at full speed: with plain inserters, the circuit and the gear on belt a with two inserters and the plate on belt b with one, an assembler makes 50.4 a minute; with fast inserters, one on each belt, a full 60. With five ingredients the oil refinery gets steel plates and gear wheels on belt a, bricks and circuits on belt b and pipes on belt c, with one inserter on each belt.

The columns that make the ingredients stand from left to right: first those of belt a, then those of belt b and those of belt c, each in the recipe's order, then the final column, with the columns of the weave just west of it. The belts run north and the products reach them from below, the first product of a belt through the lane gadget when a second one shares the belt. Each belt comes up to the tile under the first tile of the weave, so every inserter sees everything that comes up the belt. An arc of a column further west runs deeper than the arcs of the columns east of it, so belts never cross.

The weave is made of splitters and underground belts, so a blueprint weaves its belts only when your force has researched the splitter and the underground belt, the underground belt spans 5 tiles (the basic one does) and the pole reaches two rows (the small one does). Until then an item of three or four ingredients is built with the long-handed inserters described above: the blueprint is a little smaller than the woven one, and a heavy recipe takes more machines (30 assembling machines 1 a minute, with plain inserters, take 5 final machines instead of 4). Five or six ingredients need three belts, which only the weave has, with the medium electric pole as well: without them the item is refused, with a message that names what to research.

### One belt of each material

If your force has researched the splitter and the underground belt, a blueprint does not ask for a belt of raw items for every chain. It asks for **one belt for each material**, with the material on both lanes: a belt of iron ore, a belt of copper ore, a belt of coal, a belt of wood, a belt of stone, whatever the recipe needs. The blueprint divides them itself, in a band along its top:

- A furnace takes its ore on one lane and its coal on the other. Where a chain needs both, a *mixer* brings them together: a belt with nothing behind it that is fed from both sides puts the belt from the north on one lane and the one from the south on the other.
- The ore (and the wood or stone) runs along the top on *trunks*, each as full as a lane allows (450 ore a minute on a trunk that also carries coal; a trunk with one item uses both lanes). A *splitter* in front of every chain sends it its share, and the belt comes down to the chain.
- The coal, which every furnace needs, is a *bus*: one belt that comes down at the west edge and a splitter for each trunk. A second belt of the same material comes in when the trunks need more than the two lanes of one belt carry (900 a minute).
- A belt that has to cross another goes under it with an *underground belt*.

This is for every blueprint, a lone chain included: iron plates ask for a belt of iron ore and a belt of coal, where the original design asks for one belt with the ore on one lane and the coal on the other. The message names the belts that come in, from the top down, and each starts at the west edge of the blueprint in the row of its own.

The chains start under the band, which takes room: the first column starts 4 tiles from the west edge plus 4 more for each bus, and each trunk adds about 3 rows at the top. The recipe still fixes the width and the rate the height.

The design is used only when your force has unlocked the recipes of **both** the splitter and the underground belt, both exist in the game, and the underground belt spans at least 3 tiles (the basic one spans 5). Otherwise, and when no force is known, the blueprint is built the original way, as described above, and nothing is refused because of it.

### Fast belts

If your force has unlocked the recipes of the **fast transport belt, the fast splitter and the fast underground belt** (the game gives them together, with logistics 2), and the game has all three, every belt of the blueprint is a fast belt, and every splitter and underground belt a fast one. A lane of a fast belt carries twice a basic lane, 900 items a minute, so every limit that comes from a lane doubles: 900 ore on a trunk that also carries coal, 900 plates from a column of furnaces before it has to be built wide, and so on. The planner takes the speed from the belt it builds with, so nothing else changes. The three are all fast or all basic: a basic splitter or underground belt in the line of a fast belt would carry half of it. The fast underground belt spans 7 tiles, the basic one 5; the design needs at least 3 either way. The numbers in the other sections (450, 900 and the maxima that follow from them) are those of the basic belts; with the fast ones the limits that a lane sets are about twice as many (iron plate 1800, steel plate 360, electronic circuit 300), and a few others are not exactly twice, because the machines and the inserters are counted in whole numbers (heavy armor 3 instead of 1).

### Assembling machine 2

If your force has unlocked the recipe of the **assembling machine 2** and the game has it, every assembling step of the blueprint is built with it instead of the assembling machine 1: it is the same size, with a crafting speed of 0.75 where the basic one has 0.5, so a step needs a third fewer machines. The tables above name the assembling machine 1, which every blueprint is built with when the recipe is not unlocked (and when no force is known). The machine's speed comes from the game, like the belt's and the inserter's, and a maximum that a belt lane or an inserter sets does not move: a faster machine only means fewer of them. Copper cable, iron stick and transport belt, whose machines make more than one fast inserter moves, are the exception: the machine 2 gives them a second output inserter, and the belt then carries two lanes (see "Limits"), 1,800 a minute with fast belts.

### Recipes that take a fluid

Concrete takes 100 water for every 10 concrete besides its items (5 bricks and an iron ore), which only a machine with a fluid input can take: the assembling machine 2. `scripts/config.lua` names it for the category of such recipes (`crafting-with-fluid`), as the only tier, so concrete is built with it whether or not you have unlocked it (its recipe has to be unlocked, as for any item). The machines of that step face west, where their fluid connection is, so the middle row of the side with the inserters is the pipe's, and the inserters stand in the top and the bottom row only. An underground pipe starts in the tile beside each machine and goes west under the belts to the column before them, where a trunk of pipes runs along the whole column. A pipe takes the trunk up and west along the top of the blueprint to a pump in the top left corner, where the belts come in, with a pole beside it that is wired to the others. The pump's input faces west, free (the lamps leave the tile behind it free): that is where you bring the water. One pump serves up to 320 tiles of pipe, and the blueprint is refused if its pipes are more. The poles of that side stand in the columns beside the belts.

The items come on one belt, one item on each lane, with one or two inserters for every machine, or, for three or four items, on two belts woven so that every machine has one inserter on each (the pattern for machines with a pipe in `scripts/weave.lua`: belt a goes under belt b in hops of up to 5 tiles, and belt b goes in and out of the column beside the inserters in a detour of curves, so its lanes stay as they are, with no splitters). Two woven belts need the splitter and the underground belt researched, like every woven belt; without them a recipe with a fluid and more than two items is refused. The message lists the water, in units a minute, after the raw items and the fuel.

A step takes a fluid only when it takes two or more things from other steps or belts (the column of a step with one has no room beside its belt for the pipes): concrete is made from bricks (made in furnaces) and iron ore (a raw item that rides the other lane of the belt of the bricks). One fluid, at most four items, and a 3 x 3 machine that has a fluid input; anything else is refused, with a message that says which. (A recipe that takes one fluid and nothing else is another thing: its machines have no belt to take from, see "An item made from a fluid: solid fuel".)

Refined concrete takes water too, and its concrete is made in a column of its own that takes water, so a blueprint can have several columns with a trunk of pipes each. They all go to the one pump: the pipe along the top of the blueprint has a pipe up from each trunk, and where belts run over a column (the belts of raw items, or the trunks of the belts that come in) the pipe goes up under them as an underground pipe, from the tile above the trunk to the row above the blueprint, which the game allows for up to 10 tiles. The pipe along the top then stands two rows above the blueprint instead of one. A column that has more belts over it than an underground pipe spans is refused. The blueprint has no water supply, like the other raw items: bring a pipe to the input of the pump in the top left corner.

### A recipe that makes a fluid: basic oil processing

Basic oil processing is the first recipe that makes a fluid, petroleum gas. It is picked in the same picker as the others, and the rate is in units of the fluid a minute (the message names the crude oil the blueprint takes a minute). Until your force has unlocked the recipe, the message under the rate field says so.

It is made from crude oil in the oil refinery: `scripts/config.lua` lists the recipe (`recipes`) and the machine for its category, `oil-processing`. Advanced oil processing and coal liquefaction make petroleum gas too, with other inputs and other products, and are not listed. The blueprint has no belts and no inserters, and does not need the splitter or the underground belt: a column of refineries one under the other, facing east (the row is made facing north and turned a quarter clockwise), with
- a pipe along the side where the game says the refinery takes its fluid (on its west), from the north end of the blueprint to the last connection of the last refinery, so it runs past every pipe connection of every refinery: crude oil comes in at the top left corner (see the next point);
- a pipe along the opposite side, where the refinery gives its fluids (on its east), from the first connection of the first refinery to the last connection of the last: petroleum gas goes out at the south end;
- a pole beside the crude oil pipe, on its west, level with the middle of each refinery, wired to the next (the lamps of the blueprint come with them once the small lamp is researched);
- the start of every pipe you bring in is at the top left corner, where the belts you bring in start: the pipe goes up from its first tile, above the blueprint, and west along a row of its own to the west edge, where its end is open to the west for you to join. When there are two, the one that starts furthest east is on the top row and the pipes are three rows apart, so that none crosses or touches another (`scripts/corner_entries.lua`);
- a display panel beside each of these entries, above it, showing the fluid, once the display panel is researched (the same labels as the belts that come in have, `scripts/labels.lua`; the petroleum gas pipe has none), and the tile west of the entry, where you join the pipe, is kept free of lamps.

The pipes run past all the connections, the ones the recipe does not use too, so the blueprint does not depend on which of the refinery's connections basic oil processing uses (it is assumed that a connection the recipe does not use takes and gives no fluid). Where the connections are comes from the game: each pipe connection of the machine's fluid boxes is given as the tile of the machine it is on, at its edge, and the way it points (as the machine faces north), and the pipe goes on the tile beyond it. The mod refuses, each with a message of its own: a machine whose connections of what it takes are not all in one row beside it with those of what it gives in the row on the opposite side; a recipe that takes more than one fluid, or an item, or makes more than one product; a pole that does not reach two tiles or whose wire is shorter than a machine is wide. It also refuses a machine that burns fuel, or is not square. A connection the game gives in a way the mod does not understand stops it with an error that names the tiles, instead of a guess.

One blueprint has at most as many refineries as stand one under the other in the length a run of pipes may have. The game's wiki (Fluid system) says that the fluid in a pipeline segment "has no limit on its flow" and moves through it instantly, as long as the pipeline is not too long (it breaks when it spreads outside 320 x 320 tiles, unless a pump splits it), so the pipes are not what limits the refineries, only that length: the config says 320 tiles. Refineries of 5 x 5 are 64 in 320 tiles, which at crafting speed 1 make 34,560 petroleum gas a minute (540 each, from a recipe of 100 crude oil that makes 45 petroleum gas in 5 seconds); a higher rate is refused with a message that names the most. (The recipe, and with it the figures, come from the game. The other limit the wiki gives, about 4,200 units a second in practice for each connection of a machine, is far above what any refinery or plant takes in, 20 a second for a refinery of speed 1, so it is not counted.)

### An item made from a fluid: solid fuel

Solid fuel from petroleum gas takes a fluid and nothing else, and makes an item, so the blueprint makes the gas too, from crude oil, and the only thing the player brings is a pipe of crude oil. `scripts/config.lua` names the recipe that makes the fluid a recipe takes (`fluid_makers`: basic oil processing for petroleum gas), and the machine of the category `chemistry`, the chemical plant. The refineries are the column of "A recipe that makes a fluid: basic oil processing" (crude oil comes in at the north end, by the pipe west of them), and the chemical plants stand east of the pipe that takes the gas out, one under the other from the top, facing west, so that their connections for what they take are on the side of that pipe: the one pipe runs along the refineries and the plants, and brings the gas to them. An inserter stands beside the middle of the east side of each plant and drops the solid fuel on a belt that runs south beside the inserters, from the first plant to the last: the fuel leaves at the south end. A pole stands beside the top row of each plant, in the column of the inserters, wired to the next; the first is wired to the first pole of the refineries, with a pole between them above the blueprint where a wire does not reach. The crude oil pipe has a display panel beside its start once the display panel is researched, as the belts that come in have; the belt of fuel has none, as no belt that leaves the blueprint has.

The rate is in solid fuel a minute. The plants are as many as the rate needs, each making 30 a minute (a whole number makes a little more than asked), and the refineries as many as the gas of the plants needs (600 a minute each, against the 540 a refinery makes): 100 solid fuel a minute is 4 plants, which make 120, and 5 refineries. An inserter that cannot move 30 a minute holds a plant down to what it moves, which takes more plants for the rate and less gas for each. One lane of the belt carries what 15 plants make, 450 a minute (the fast belt twice that), and the length a run of pipes may have (64 refineries and 106 plants of 3 x 3 in 320 tiles, which is never the smaller of the two for these machines) is the other limit: a higher rate is refused with a message that names the most. The message names the crude oil the blueprint takes a minute. Only the recipe of the solid fuel has to be unlocked, as for any item.

It is refused, with the messages of the row of refineries, when the recipe takes more than one fluid or anything else or makes more than one product, when the recipe that makes the fluid does not make it from one fluid, when the pipe connections of a machine are not in rows beside it as the layout draws them, or when the pole does not reach the inserter beside a plant or its wire the next plant; and with "no recipe makes" the fluid when the config names no recipe for it (the recipes of solid fuel from light oil and heavy oil, whose oils the mod does not make, are not offered).

### A second fluid: sulfur

Sulfur takes petroleum gas and water, 30 of each for 2 sulfur in 1 second. Crude oil and water are raw, like the ores: they come in by a pipe at the top left corner of the blueprint (the crude oil on the row nearest the blueprint and the water three rows above it), each with a display panel showing the fluid once the force has unlocked the panel, and the refineries make the petroleum gas as for solid fuel (a plant takes 1,800 gas a minute at crafting speed 1, which is more than three refineries make, so there are more refineries than plants). The recipe says which fluid is made by the machines of the row (the one `fluid_makers` names a recipe for) and which comes in; a recipe that takes two fluids that both have a recipe, or three fluids, is refused.

A chemical plant takes one fluid at its top row and the other at its bottom row (as it faces west), so two columns of pipes stand between the pipe that takes the gas out of the refineries and the plants, and every second plant is mirrored, so that the bottom row of a plant and the top row of the next take the same fluid. The column next to the plants has a pipe at the rows of both fluids; the pipes of the water are joined by underground pipes, which go under the rows of the gas; the pipes of the gas have a pipe beside them in the column that is beside the pipe of the refineries, which joins them to it. The inserters, the belt and the poles are east of the plants as for solid fuel. The layout agrees, pipe for pipe, with the one the owner built by hand for four plants (`spec/fluid_plant_spec.lua` has it).

Which input box takes which fluid is the recipe's `fluidbox_index` when it has one and the order of the fluids when it does not; the first plant is mirrored when its first-row box would take the gas, so that the water enters at the top. The rate is in sulfur a minute, with the plants, the refineries and the limits as for solid fuel (a plant makes 120 a minute at crafting speed 1, held down to what its output inserter moves, which takes less gas and water for it); the message names the crude oil and the water the blueprint takes a minute. It is refused, with the message of the pipes, when the two input boxes of the chemical plant are not one connection each at its top and bottom rows or the recipe does not put its fluids in two different boxes, and with the missing prototype when the game has no underground pipe.

### An item and a fluid: plastic bar

Plastic bar takes 1 coal and 20 petroleum gas for 2 plastic bars in 1 second. The gas comes from refineries as for solid fuel. The coal is raw, like an ore: its belt comes in at the top left corner, at the west edge, on the top row (the crude oil pipe is three rows under it), and goes east above the blueprint and south down a column beside the plants, with a display panel showing coal beside its entry. A recipe with one item that is made from other items (not raw) is not laid out this way, and neither is one with an item and two fluids.

The belt of coal is on the west side of the plants, where their pipe connections are, as the concrete columns have their belts (see "Concrete"): from the pipe that takes the gas out of the refineries east are a column where underground pipes start, the belt of coal (running south from the top left corner down the plants), and a column of inserters beside the plants. At each pipe connection of each plant an underground pipe goes from the tile beside the plant, under the belt, to the column by the gas pipe, so the gas reaches the plants under the coal; an inserter at the row of each plant that has no connection takes the coal from the belt into the plant, and a pole in the column of the underground pipes powers it (wired to the others). East of the plants everything is as for solid fuel: an inserter at the middle of each plant drops the plastic on a belt that runs south. The game must have the underground pipe, which the blueprint is refused without.

The rate is in plastic bars a minute. A plant makes 120 a minute at crafting speed 1, held down to what its output inserter moves and to what the inserter that brings the coal moves (a plastic bar takes half a coal), whichever is less; the plants are as many as the rate needs, the refineries as many as the gas of the plants needs, and the limits are those of solid fuel (a lane of the output belt and the length of the pipes) and what the belt of coal carries, both of whose lanes carry coal. The message names the crude oil and the coal the blueprint takes a minute, and the belt that comes in.

### Steel furnace

If your force has unlocked the recipe of the **steel furnace** and the game has it, every furnace of the blueprint (the plates, the bricks and the steel) is a steel furnace instead of a stone furnace: the same 2 x 2 tiles and the same fuel, at twice the crafting speed, so a column needs half as many furnaces (75 iron plates a minute take 2 furnaces, not 4). The most a blueprint makes does not change, since a lane of a belt sets it; the blueprints get smaller.

### Steel

Steel plate is five iron plates smelted in a furnace, so it is a furnace that eats what other furnaces make and burns coal. Its coal cannot come from a belt of raw items, so the belt of plates that runs from the iron furnaces to the steel furnaces carries it: the iron furnaces' inserters put the plates on one lane of that belt, and the coal rides the other. The coal comes down from the band beside the output inserters of the iron furnaces and turns east into the tile above the head of the belt, which has a stub of belt behind it so that it stays straight and the side-load puts the coal on the lane the plates leave free. The belt of plates runs south, so that its head, where the coal comes in, is at the top. The coal is one more feed for the band, a trunk of its own that carries only coal.

This needs the design that brings in one belt of each material, so steel plate, steel chests and heavy armor are refused, with a message, when the splitter and the underground belt are not researched. A furnace in the middle of a chain can eat only one made ingredient (the plates), because the coal takes the other lane. This is the design up to 90 steel plates a minute; above that, with the medium electric pole unlocked, the furnaces are built wide (see "Furnaces built wide"), and the steel furnaces take their coal on a belt of their own instead.

**Not seen in game yet.** The specs trace every lane of every blueprint, for every item at every whole rate, but a trace follows the rules assumed below, which only the game can confirm: a splitter keeps the lanes of what goes through it and divides each lane evenly; a belt that is fed from both sides and has nothing behind it puts what comes from each side on its own lane; a belt tile fed from the side by the output of a splitter bends and keeps the lanes; an underground belt keeps the lanes of what goes through it, even with an entrance right after a belt, an exit right before the next entrance, or a hop under the output of a splitter. The coal of the steel furnaces side-loading the near lane of a belt that already carries plates on its far lane, from a tile with a stub behind it, is assumed the same way, and so is the steel furnaces' inserters taking both items from that belt. The lane gadget of the columns (described above) has not been seen running in game either. The same goes for the woven input belts of machines with more than two ingredients: a splitter peeling a share of a belt off onto a dead-end tile that an inserter takes from (the belt keeps both lanes, nothing blocks the other lane when one is full), an underground belt that goes under a piece of another underground belt that crosses it, an underground belt that comes up right before the next one goes down, and the inserters taking from those tiles.

### How the columns are found

The table only says which items you can ask for. The steps come from the game's recipes: the mod looks up the recipe that makes the item, then the recipes for the ingredients that are themselves made, and so on until the ingredients are raw (no recipe it can build makes them, like ores and stone). The machine of each step comes from the recipe's category, set in `scripts/config.lua` (smelting: stone furnace burning coal; crafting: assembling machine 1). A request is refused, with a message, when:

- no recipe the mod can build makes the item, or more than one does (it cannot choose between them yet);
- a step has more than six ingredients that are themselves made (three belts have six lanes, one for each);
- a step has made ingredients and more than six ingredients in all, or more than one raw item (a belt of raw items fills both lanes, so it takes a lane of its own and only one is laid out), or has one made ingredient and a raw one, the made one itself coming from two or more inputs (a column that takes two things on top of a block with several inputs is not laid out yet);
- a step that is fed by other steps needs fuel. The raw items and the fuel ride on the belts of the steps that are fed raw items, with one exception: a furnace that eats the one product of the step before it (steel plate from iron plates) takes its fuel on the free lane of that belt, when the splitter and the underground belt are researched (see "Steel");
- a recipe makes more than one product, or has a fluid among its ingredients in a step that cannot take one (see "Recipes that take a fluid").

### Lanes and inserters

(This describes the original design, with a belt of raw items for every chain. In the design that brings in one belt of each material, the lanes are made inside the blueprint: see "One belt of each material".) Each belt of raw items has two lanes (the belts come in from the west edge, one row each at the top, in the order of the tables above, the first one on the top row): an ingredient and the fuel get a lane each (furnaces), an ingredient with no fuel gets both lanes, and a recipe with two ingredients and no fuel gets one lane each. A step fed raw items that needs more items than two lanes hold (two ingredients and a fuel, or three ingredients) is refused. An inserter drops items on the far lane of a belt, so a plain output belt carries the product on one lane only. That is the case for every belt between two columns, and the next column picks the product up from it. The output belt of the last column can wind and fill both lanes: a 3 x 3 machine with two output inserters gets a winding output belt (see below).

(With the fast inserter researched a side may use fast ones: see "Fast inserters".) Each side of a machine gets as many inserters as the machine needs, up to one fewer than it is wide: one beside a 2 x 2 furnace, two beside a 3 x 3 assembling machine (output side: at most 2, whatever the size). That keeps one tile beside every machine free for a pole, so the machines sit side by side with no gaps. (The plain and the long-handed inserters of a second input belt count together. The input side of a 3 x 3 machine that takes from woven belts is the exception: it may get three, since its poles stand beside the belts; see "Recipes with more than two ingredients".) A machine that would need more inserters than that simply runs slower, and the blueprint is sized for it: an assembling machine 1 making gear wheels would eat 120 plates per minute and make 60 gears, but two basic inserters move about 100 plates, so each machine makes about 50 gears per minute. The message shows the real total. A column with a single machine that is used for only part of its speed gets only the inserters that share needs: asking for 1 copper cable per minute builds an assembling machine with one input and one output inserter (and no winding belt), not two output inserters. With two or more machines the first ones run flat out and keep every inserter. The inserter's speed comes from the game data.

### The winding output belt

When a 3 x 3 machine in the last column needs two output inserters, the output belt is two tiles from the machine side and winds between two columns, the inner one beside the inserters and the outer one beside it. The belt has one seam for every two output inserters in adjacent rows (a machine's two inserters, with plain ones), in two rows: at a seam the belt comes down in the inner column and curves east, where the first inserter drops on the curve (one lane); it runs on in the outer column, which a short stub from the second inserter side-loads (the other lane). The next seam does the same the other way round: the stub of the first inserter side-loads the belt coming down in the outer column, and in the second row the belt turns west into the inner column, where the second inserter drops on the curve. So each machine fills both lanes. The belt starts in the inner column one tile above the first curve: without a belt behind it Factorio would bend the first stub into a curve and its lane would not work. Machines with one output inserter keep the plain belt (unless it is a fast one, see "Fast inserters"), and so does every column that feeds another one.

### Machines in pairs

The machines of a column go in twos: the inserters of the first, third, ... machine stand in its top rows, those of the second, fourth, ... machine in its bottom rows, so that the second machine of a pair and the first of the next have theirs together. A machine with no partner, the last of an odd number, is arranged like the first (in a column of five, machines 1 and 5 look alike). The long-handed inserters are part of the pattern: a machine with one fast and one long-handed input inserter has them as fast, long in its top rows and as long, fast in its bottom rows, so the rows read fast, long, long, fast for a pair. The woven input belts are part of the pattern too: the belt that the rows of the inserter column take from reads a, b, c for the first machine of a pair and c, b, a for the second (see "Recipes with more than two ingredients"), so the two machines show the same belt where they meet. It is the same on every belt, the belt between two columns included, so the inserters of two columns that share a belt agree. The winding belt is drawn around the output inserters standing together. The poles follow the inserters: the machines go from the top down, each with a pole in the free tile under its inserters (above them for a machine whose inserters are in its bottom rows) unless a pole above it reaches them, which a small pole does two rows each way and a medium pole three (see "Medium electric poles").

The belt between two columns runs south unless the first column is the longer one for the work it does and its last drop is at or below the next column's last pickup: items flow only the way the belt runs, so on a northbound belt a pickup below the last drop would never see a plate. In that rare case (one furnace and one assembler with two input inserters, for example) the belt runs south, whatever the load rule says.

### Lights

Once your force has researched the small lamp, every blueprint gets lamps. They stand on free tiles inside the supply area of a pole, so each one is powered by the poles that power the machines, with no wire of its own. Two rules place them:

- **Hard: no two lamps are closer than 10 tiles.**
- **Soft: every tile a pole powers has a lamp within 10 tiles.**

The tiles a pole powers are taken from the top row down, left to right. Where a tile has no lamp within 10 tiles, one is put on the closest free tile that is at least 10 tiles from every lamp so far, which is the tile itself when it is free. So the hard rule holds for every blueprint, and the soft rule fails only where no free tile is left near a powered tile (a few tiles in a thousand between crowded machines, in the sweep of every recipe at every rate). A tile with something on it is not free, and neither is the tile a belt runs on to, so that you can go on with the belts that leave the blueprint. Without the small lamp researched, or in a game that does not have it, blueprints have no lamps.

### Display panels

Once your force has researched the display panel, a blueprint that brings in one belt of each material (see "One belt of each material") gets a display panel beside each belt you bring in, showing the item that belt carries, so it is easy to see which resource goes where. A panel stands on the tile above the first tile of the belt, or under it when that tile is taken, or west of those two as a last try. It never stands between two belts that come in (it would not say which one it labels), on a tile a belt runs on to, or behind the pump of the water inlet. A belt with no free tile of those four goes without a panel, which happens where belts come in on neighbouring rows (about one belt in sixteen in the sweep of every product). The panels need no power.

### Fast inserters

Once your force has researched the fast inserter, a machine side that one plain inserter cannot serve gets fast ones. A side goes up a ladder as the machine's flow grows: one plain inserter (50.4 items a minute), one fast inserter (144), two fast inserters (288), never two plain ones, since one fast inserter moves nearly three. Each side climbs on its own, so a machine can eat through two fast inserters and empty through one plain one. On woven input belts (see "Recipes with more than two ingredients") every inserter of the side is a fast one as soon as one belt needs more than a plain inserter moves, as many on each belt as it needs. The long-handed inserters of a second input belt that is not woven stay as they are.

A side still takes at most one fewer inserter than the machine is wide, two on a 3x3 machine, which keeps a tile beside it free for a pole; a machine that needs more than two fast inserters move runs slower. The planner counts what the best inserter moves when it decides which ingredients ride which belt (the fast inserter, 144 a minute, against 50.4 for a plain one and 72 for a long-handed one): with woven belts the inserter's assembler takes its circuits and gears on belt a (120 of the 144) and the plates on belt b, one fast inserter each, and runs at full speed; with plain inserters it takes two on belt a and one on belt b and makes 50.4 a minute. With a long-handed inserter on belt b the same assembler takes its circuits alone on belt a and the gears and plates on belt b, and makes 36 a minute; with a fast inserter on belt a it takes circuits and gears on belt a and the plates on belt b, and runs at full speed too. A machine runs at full speed more often, so fewer machines are built.

A machine that makes more than one plain inserter moves gets one fast output inserter, which moves all of it, and the last column winds its belt with it on each 3x3 machine (with plain inserters a machine gets two output inserters, so the product rides both lanes: 900 a minute). The first machine's inserter stands alone in its top rows and drops on a curve of the belt (the outer lane); after it, the second machine's inserter and the third's stand side by side, one dropping on a stub that side-loads the belt (the inner lane) and the other on the curve where the belt turns west (the outer lane); the belt hops back to the outer column between the seams, so every machine takes the other lane than the one before: outer, inner, outer, inner and so on. The product keeps both lanes with half the inserters. A lane gets half the machines, and one more for an odd number, which has the last machine, the only one that may run for part of its time. A lane that carries 3.75 machines' worth (copper cable's machines make 120 a minute) takes 3 whole machines, and 0.75 of the seventh if there is one, so the belt counts for 6.75 machines' worth: copper cable tops out at 810 a minute with fast inserters, and at 900 without them. (With a whole number of machines' worth on a lane the belt carries exactly twice that, both lanes full.)

Without the fast inserter researched (or when the game does not have it), every blueprint is built with plain inserters. (The figures of this section and of the ones before are for an inserter that moves one item in a swing; see "Inserter capacity bonus" for what the research changes.)

### Inserter capacity bonus

Once your force has researched the inserter capacity bonus, an inserter moves more than one item in a swing: one, the bonus built into the inserter (none, for the plain, the long-handed and the fast inserter) and the research of the force, which the game gives as a number and the mod counts in whole items (a hand of two items for the first level). Without a force, or without the research, an inserter moves one item, and every figure in this README is for that.

The bigger hand costs time at the belt: an inserter holds still for each item after the first. On the way in it waits for the next item to come along the belt to it, and on the way out for the item it has just dropped to be carried clear of the place of the next. Each wait is the time a full lane takes to carry one item past a point, 8 ticks on a basic belt (7.5 items a second) and 4 on a fast belt, so a faster belt costs an inserter less, and the belts that count are those of the blueprint. An inserter moves hand x 3600 / (time to fully rotate + (hand - 1) x wait) items a minute, the times in ticks. A swing takes 1 / rotation speed ticks (71.43 for a plain inserter, 50 for a long-handed one, 25 for a fast one), so with a hand of two a plain inserter moves 2 items in 71.43 + 8 ticks, 90.6 items a minute on basic belts (50.4 with a hand of one) and 95.5 on fast belts; a long-handed inserter 124.1 (72 with a hand of one) and 133.3, a fast inserter 218.2 (144) and 248.3. The input side and the output side use the same figures, and the planner counts with them wherever it counted 50.4, 72 and 144 before.

A side then needs fewer inserters, a machine runs at full speed more often, so fewer machines are built, and a side that one plain inserter now moves does not get a fast one. An assembler that eats 180 plates a minute takes one fast inserter (218.2) where it took two (144 each). The product of a machine that makes 60 a minute (gear wheels, pipes) was moved by a fast inserter, since a plain one with a hand of one moves 50.4, and is now moved by a plain one, so the output belt is a plain belt with the product on one lane, where the fast inserter wound the belt so that the product rode both lanes (see "Fast inserters"): the lane then limits the blueprint, and pipes top out at 450 a minute on basic belts instead of 870.

Not counted yet: a lane that carries less than a full lane (an inserter then waits longer for the next item), the items of other inserters on the belt (which can hold an inserter up on the way out), and that an inserter takes one kind of item in a swing.

### Medium electric poles

Once your force has unlocked the medium electric pole, every blueprint is built with medium poles instead of small ones. Without it unlocked, or in a game that does not have it, the poles are small. How far a pole powers and how long a wire it holds come from the game: a small pole powers 2.5 tiles from its centre (a 5 x 5 area) and holds a wire of 7.5 tiles, a medium pole 3.5 (7 x 7) and 9. The poles stand in the free tile beside a machine; what changes is how far each one reaches. A medium pole beside the first of three furnaces also powers the inserters of the other two, where a small pole's two rows stop short of the third, and one pole on each side powers both machines of a pair of 3 x 3 machines that have two inserters on a side. The nearest poles of two columns are also within a wire's reach more often, so fewer poles are needed between them.

**A column of 2 x 2 machines (the furnaces) has its poles on the output side alone.** The input inserters are three tiles from a pole in the free tile beside the output inserters, which a medium pole reaches, so that one pole powers the input inserters, the machine and the output inserters, and the input side needs none (a small pole reaches two tiles, so a small pole keeps a pole on each side). A column of furnaces takes about half the poles it did: 450 iron plates a minute from 12 poles to 6, 90 steel plates from 24 to 12 (with the fast belts, the assembling machine 2 and the steel furnace; the columns of 3 x 3 machines keep their poles on both sides, since a medium pole reaches only 3 tiles and their input inserters are 4 away). This is the same arrangement that the furnaces built wide use, whose input side is full.

**The poles on both sides of a belt that two columns share are not both needed.** The output inserters of one column and the input inserters of the next stand on either side of the belt between them. When the poles beside the output inserters of the first column reach every input inserter of the next across the belt (two tiles away for a plain belt, which a small pole reaches, three for the winding belt of furnaces built wide, which a medium pole reaches), the next column has no input poles. If one inserter of it is out of reach, it keeps all of them.

A side still takes at most one inserter fewer than the machine is wide, so that a tile beside every machine stays free for a pole (the input side of machines that take from woven belts has its poles beside the belts).

### Furnaces built wide

A column of furnaces makes at most what one lane of a belt carries: the ore rides one lane of its belt (the coal the other), so 450 ore a minute, 24 stone furnaces and 450 plates a minute, whose lane the plates then ride. When you ask for more than that, and your force has the splitter, the underground belt and the medium electric pole unlocked, the furnaces are built wide:

- **A second input belt**, one column west of the first, carries the ore on both lanes. The two belts are woven so that two plain inserters on every furnace reach both: in the column beside the inserters the first belt (ore on one lane, coal on the other) goes under for two rows, and in those two rows the second belt comes into that column and goes back (an underground entrance, two tiles, an exit, and the next entrance right under the exit; the second belt only turns, so its lanes stay as they are). Every furnace takes the first belt with one inserter and the second with the other, in mirrored order for the second furnace of a pair. That is three lanes of ore and one of coal. A column with an odd number of furnaces ends in an exit two rows under its last inserter. Both belts are made inside the blueprint from the belt of ore the player brings in, which the band splits between them (a blueprint whose ore passes what a belt carries, 900 a minute, asks for another belt).
- **The output belt winds in twos** (see "The winding output belt" and "Fast inserters"): every furnace has one output inserter and the furnaces alternate lanes, so the plates ride both lanes. A lane carries 24 furnaces' worth (450 plates a minute), so the belt carries 2 x 24 = 48, 24 on each lane. The belt winds also when it feeds another column, which takes the plates from the outer column of the winding belt; it runs north or south as the plates need (see "Machines in pairs"), the north one being the south one reflected.
- **The poles stand on the output side**, in the free tile of every other furnace (one pole for each two), and a medium pole reaches the input inserters from there, three tiles away; the input side is full, so no tile beside it is free.

Plates then top out at 900 a minute (48 furnaces) instead of 450, and what is made from them gains as much where the next stage takes the plates on both lanes: gear wheels 450 instead of 225, pipes 870 instead of 450, transport belts 810 instead of 450. A brick takes two stone, so three lanes of stone feed 36 furnaces: 675 bricks instead of 225. Steel plate goes from 90 to 180 a minute (48 steel furnaces; see below), and steel chests from 11 to 22, barrels from 90 to 180. Copper cable and iron stick make more than before too (780 to 810, because of the belt of the fast inserters). Only a stage that is fed raw items, burns fuel and eats one ingredient is built wide, and only when it is the last stage of the blueprint or its plates go to a stage that eats nothing else and burns no fuel (gears from iron plates, copper cable from copper plates: the stage then takes the plates on both lanes). A column of furnaces whose plates go to a stage with a second ingredient (a circuit's iron plates), or that feeds another column through an arc, stays plain. A request that the plain design makes is built plain. The original design, without the splitter or the underground belt, never builds furnaces wide.

**Steel furnaces after furnaces built wide** are built wide too, since the plates then fill both lanes of the belt between the two columns and the coal has no lane left. The steel furnaces take their coal on a belt of their own, woven with the belt of plates in the same way: a new column beside the steel furnaces' inserters holds the coal's underground belt, which comes down from the top of the blueprint straight into its first entrance, and the plates' winding belt (its outer column) turns east into that column and back for two rows of every four. The winding belt is straight in exactly those rows (the output inserters of the iron furnaces are in the other two), so the two patterns never meet. Each steel furnace takes the coal with one inserter and the plates with the other, and there are as many steel furnaces as iron furnaces, since a steel furnace eats 18.75 plates a minute, as much as an iron furnace makes. The belt of plates runs south, as for the plain design. The steel furnaces start one furnace lower than the iron furnaces: a plate only goes the way the belt runs, and a steel furnace takes its plates from one tile (the detour), so the plates of the lowest iron furnace, which drop in the last row, would pass every steel furnace if they started level. Lower, the plates of every iron furnace reach an inserter of a steel furnace, and each steel furnace has the plates of at least as many iron furnaces as there are steel furnaces up to it. The belt of the steel then runs north, up to the first row of the blueprint, for the next column's first inserters.

## Limits

- **A lane carries 450 items per minute.** (Furnaces built wide get past this limit for plates, and for what is made from them: see "Furnaces built wide". The numbers below are those of the plain design.) The product travels on one lane (plain belt) or two (winding belt, two output inserters), so one blueprint makes at most 450 or 900 per minute, depending on what the machines can move, and the ingredients get one lane each, or both lanes (900 per minute) when one ingredient has the belt to itself. A stone furnace makes 18.75 plates or bricks per minute, so a plate blueprint has at most 24 furnaces. A higher rate is refused with a message that names the most one blueprint can make. Splitting a bigger request into several blueprints is not supported.
- **Every column has its own limit.** Each column must make what the next one needs for the rate asked (asking for 1 gear wheel per minute builds 1 furnace and 1 assembling machine, since 2 plates are all it takes), and the belt between two columns is a plain belt that carries one item on one lane, so the limit of every column counts and the lowest one wins. Gear wheels: each assembling machine eats about 100.8 plates per minute (two basic inserters move that much, see above), so one lane of plates carries about 4.5 machines' worth. The limit follows the belts, not whole machines: a lane that carries 4.5 machines' worth feeds 5 machines, and the 5th simply runs part of the time. One blueprint makes at most 225 gears per minute (24 furnaces and 5 assemblers; the message names the exact number). Asking for 100 gears per minute builds 11 furnaces and 2 assemblers.
- **Electronic circuits: at most 150 per minute.** A circuit takes three cables, and the cables ride one lane (450 a minute). Two input inserters move 100.8 items a minute, and a circuit eats four, so a circuit assembler makes 25.2 per minute (6 assemblers for 150).
- **Recipes that eat a lot hit the limit sooner.** A brick takes two stone, so each furnace eats 37.5 stone per minute and the stone lane fills first: at most 12 furnaces and 225 bricks per minute. With two output inserters on a machine the winding belt puts one on each lane, and the machines are counted for the two lanes together: an inserter that finds its lane full waits, and the other inserter, on the other lane, takes the rest, so it does not matter which lane fills first. That is an assumption, not yet checked in game.
- **The rate is rounded up to whole machines.** Asking for 100 plates per minute builds 6 furnaces, which make 112.5 per minute. The message shows the 112.5, what the machines make, and the raw items it needs for that.
- **The winding belt needs a 3 x 3 machine, in the last column, or furnaces built wide.** A 2 x 2 machine has room for one output inserter only, and a column that feeds another one keeps a plain belt, unless its furnaces are built wide (see "Furnaces built wide").
- **Machines run on electricity or burn fuel.** The mod reads which from the machine in the game, so a machine from another mod works if it fits. The fuel the mod puts on the input belt is still set in `scripts/config.lua`; the mod checks that the machine can burn it (and that an electric machine has none set), and refuses with a message if not. A machine that runs on heat or a fluid is refused.
- **Machines are 2 x 2 or 3 x 3.** Any other size, or a machine that is not square, is refused.
- **You supply the inputs.** The blueprint contains no mining, no ore sources and no coal supply, only the machines and belts themselves. Two steps that need the same ore (a pipe and a gear for an offshore pump) each take a belt of their own (in the design that brings in one belt of each material they share the belt of that ore, up to two lanes of it).
- **You supply the power.** The poles are connected to each other, not to your network.
- **Only the items you have unlocked.** The picker leaves out an item when its own recipe is not unlocked yet for your force, and a request for one anyway (it cannot be made from the window, but the generator checks) is refused with the name of the recipe that is missing. A recipe the game does not have at all is not held against you.
- **Only the items in the table above.** Other items are refused, and so is an item whose recipes the mod cannot chain (see "How the columns are found").
- **Machines are chosen per recipe category, not per item.** `scripts/config.lua` names one machine for each category (smelting, crafting, advanced crafting and crafting with fluid for now), and an intermediate step uses the machine of its own recipe's category. A category lists its machines as tiers, from the one every blueprint can count on to the best, and a force gets the last tier it has unlocked: the assembling machine 2 for crafting (see "Assembling machine 2") and the steel furnace for smelting (see "Steel furnace"). The poles and the belts are tiers in the same way (the medium pole; the fast belt with its splitter and underground belt). The electric furnace is a later feature.
- **The limits follow the game.** The belt limit, the machine speed and the number of inserters are worked out from the current game data (and the items an inserter moves in a swing from your force's research, see "Inserter capacity bonus"), so a mod that changes the transport belt, the machine or the inserter changes the blueprint.

## Running the tests

The tests run outside the game with [busted](https://lunarmodules.github.io/busted/), a Lua testing tool. They check that the generator's logic is consistent. They cannot show that Factorio accepts a blueprint; only loading it in the game does that.

1. Install Lua and LuaRocks (Lua 5.2 is the closest match to Factorio's Lua), then run `luarocks install busted`. On Windows, WSL or Docker is the easy route.
2. From the repository root, run `busted`.

The settings are in `.busted`: specs are the `spec/*_spec.lua` files, and `spec/helper.lua` provides stand-ins for the game's `defines` table before they run. Specs for modules that read the game's `prototypes` table assign a fake one first.

A second check, `lua52 verify/run.lua` from the repository root (about 13 seconds), generates 1,329 blueprints from the game's real recipes (copied into `verify/recipes.lua`) and runs the validator and the belt tracer on them; see `.agents/verification.md`.

## Editor setup (VS Code)

The code carries type annotations (`---@param`, `---@class`, ...) so the editor can flag a wrong argument or a misspelled field as you type. They are plain comments: Factorio ignores them and nothing is checked while the game runs.

1. Install the [EmmyLua](https://marketplace.visualstudio.com/items?itemName=tangzx.emmylua) extension (`tangzx.emmylua`) and, if you have the other popular Lua extension (sumneko), disable it for this workspace.
2. Install the [Factorio Modding Toolkit](https://marketplace.visualstudio.com/items?itemName=justarandomgeek.factoriomod-debug) and pick your Factorio version when it asks. It supplies the types of Factorio's own API (`game`, `LuaPlayer`, `defines`, ...).
3. `.emmyrc.json` in this repository sets the Lua version. If the editor shows problems the code should not have, run `Factorio: Check Config` from the command palette.

The shared types of this mod are in `scripts/types.lua`; their names start with `bpgen.`.

Where things are: `control.lua` only registers the events; `scripts/gui.lua` is the window; `scripts/generator.lua` is the one place that reads the game's recipes and machines and connects them to the planner and the layout; `scripts/layout.lua` builds the columns of one chain and `scripts/rows.lua` puts the columns of a whole recipe side by side; `scripts/messages.lua` holds all the wording (with the texts themselves in `locale/en/blueprint-generation.cfg`), so a change to what the player reads can be reviewed apart from a change to what the mod builds.
