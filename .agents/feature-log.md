5e. Two layout savings, found by the owner on the offshore pump, 2026-11-07, NOT yet run in game. (1) The lower block starts in the join row instead of the row below it: its first output inserter drops onto the tile that turns east into the input belt, a curve, which side-loads everything onto one lane anyway (the tracer's curve rule, the owner's in-game rule, makes the drop land on the inside lane, which does not matter after a side-load). The belt of a raw-item block (wood) then IS the turn tile. (2) The upper block is never moved: it stays in the corner, its product belt goes straight on as the input belt, and the LOWER block is moved so that its product belt is one column to the left of it, east when it is narrower (as before) and WEST when it is as wide or wider, which puts it in negative columns. Before, equally wide blocks forced the upper block one column east, which gave its belt of raw items a trail (`>>` at the top), pushed the lower block's trail one column further west and made the input belt jog east (`v>vv`). A first attempt kept the upper block in place and turned its belt east in its last row; the owner pointed out that moving the lower block west removes the jog altogether, so that code was deleted (`blocks.junction_of` is now two lines). A lower block in negative columns needs no turn in its trail: `add_trails` puts the trail in the belt's own column when that is west of the other trails (`math.min(entry_column - 1, belt.column)`), so the trail is just a longer belt. The offshore pump is 19 columns wide instead of 22 and one row shorter. The blueprint is no longer in a frame whose x = 0 is its west side: a lower block much wider than the upper one sticks out to the west (7 columns for 4 furnaces above a two-level tree), and the overall width does not grow. Checked with the real 2.1 recipes through the validator and the tracer at every whole rate for ten products (about 2,000 blueprints). Tests: 773 busted examples; 20 breakages of the join and trail code are all caught (one breakage of an earlier version, the old block height formula, survived at first and led to a test with a tree whose lower block is its tallest part). A test that could never fail was found and repaired: "adds no poles to the last block of the blueprint" looked for a pole at column 21 of a blueprint 18 columns wide; it now looks at (16, 4), where a line of poles for the last block would be. The old test of the wider upper block was in another describe and is replaced by the describe "two blocks of which the upper is wider".

5i. A short message after generating (owner request 2026-11-08: "all I need to know are the actual number of final product / min, and the number of each raw material / min. Make it more compact and less wordy"), NOT yet run in game. The message is now two lines, `Ready: 75 Iron plate/min` and `Raw/min: 75 Iron ore, 5.4 Coal` (locale keys `generated` and `raw-amount`; the line break is `\n` in the cfg). The steps, the machines and the lane/belt instructions are GONE from it (the owner asked for only the two numbers; the order of the belts and the lanes are in the README now). The product is what the machines really make (`plan.output_per_minute`, which can be more than asked). The raw items are worked out top-down from that: the root makes so much, so many crafts, so much of each ingredient; a step that makes an ingredient is asked for that much in turn (`add_demand` in `generator.lua`), so a column of furnaces rounded up to whole machines is not charged for what it could make but will not be asked for. The fuel is worked out from the machine: crafts x recipe time / (60 x crafting speed) = machines working, times `fuel_use`, which is 3600 x the machine's `get_max_energy_usage()` / (the fuel's `fuel_value` x the burner's `effectivity`). The ores come first, in the order the recipe needs them, then the fuel. UNVERIFIED IN GAME: that `get_max_energy_usage()` is in joules per TICK (the Lua docs do not say; the tests assume 1500 for a stone furnace, 90 kW, and so 1.35 coal a minute for a furnace that never stops). If the numbers for coal look 60 times too big or too small, that is the assumption. When the game gives no energy use, the fuel is left out of the message. The blueprint's description field (the machines used, step by step) was not touched. `describe`-style tests of the old message are replaced by tests of the numbers, worked out by hand.
